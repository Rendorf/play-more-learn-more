# Vision and users

**Who this is for:** the owner, new team members, and investors, partners and schools who want to know why the game exists and who it is for.
**Last checked against:** BUILD 2026-10-25 18:32 UTC.

## The problem

Many children find maths hard, and practice at home is often a fight. Worksheets are dull. Most maths apps either drill without teaching, or are built for a different curriculum, or keep children busy without checking that they really learned anything. Parents rarely know which topics their child is stuck on.

Families who follow the **Cambridge curriculum** (Primary Stages 1–6 and Lower Secondary Stages 7–9) have even fewer good options. Few games follow that curriculum skill by skill.

## What Play More Learn More is

A free, playful maths practice game for the Cambridge curriculum, Grades 1–9. It runs in any web browser on phones, tablets and computers, and it keeps working without internet once it is open.

- **It teaches, then practises.** Every skill has a short picture lesson, then practice with hints that get more helpful after each wrong try.
- **It checks real learning.** A skill is only "mastered" after the child gets 8 of their last 10 answers right first time, on 2 different days.
- **It feels like a game.** Combos, sounds, boss battles, coins, a pet, a sticker album, streaks and four worlds to choose from.
- **It brings the family in.** Parents set real-life wishes (a trip, a book) that unlock as the child masters skills. Siblings fill a shared weekly team goal together.

It covers 48 units and 201 skills. See the [curriculum map](curriculum-map.md).

## Who it is for

| Group | Age or role | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| **Children** | About 6–15 (Grade 1 is about age 6, Grade 9 about age 14) | Short, fun sessions; help when stuck; a reason to come back tomorrow |
| **Parents and carers** | Usually the person who installs the game | Know what their child is learning and where they struggle; reward effort without nagging; feel safe about privacy |
| **Teachers and schools** | Cambridge-curriculum schools and tutors | Practice that matches what is taught in class, by stage and skill |

Today the game is in a **friends-and-family test**: families, not schools, are the users. Schools are a later possibility (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)).

## Personas

These are made-up people that stand for typical users. They are not real families.

**Leyla, 7, Grade 2.** Loves animals and stickers, reads slowly. She needs big pictures, the read-aloud button and very small steps. She plays best for five minutes before dinner. A pet that grows when she plays and a chest to open each day bring her back.

**Tom, 11, Grade 6.** Quick, competitive and easily bored. He skips lessons and wants to see his name climb the leaderboard. He needs "Find my level" to jump past what he already knows, boss battles to prove himself, and the 60-second challenge.

**Sara, parent of two.** Works full time. Her children use two tablets. She wants one place to see both children's progress, set a reward for each, and get a nudge on days nobody has played. She does not want her children chatting with strangers or seeing ads.

**Mr Hughes, Grade 4 teacher (future).** Teaches the Cambridge Primary curriculum. He wants homework practice that matches this week's topic and a simple view of which pupils are stuck. The game does not serve him yet.

## Jobs to be done

- **Child:** "When I have a few minutes, I want a short, fun round that I can win, so I feel clever and earn something."
- **Child:** "When I am stuck, I want a clue, not the answer, so I can still get it myself."
- **Parent:** "When my child is practising, I want to see which topics they find hard, so I can help or talk to the teacher."
- **Parent:** "When my child works hard for weeks, I want to give a real reward they chose with me, so effort feels worth it."
- **Parent:** "On busy days, I want a gentle reminder to reach my child, so practice becomes a habit without me nagging."
- **Teacher (later):** "When I teach a topic, I want practice that matches it exactly, so homework reinforces the lesson."

## What we deliberately don't do

- **No ads and no in-app purchases.** Coins are earned only by playing and can't be bought with money.
- **No chat or messages between children.** The leaderboard shows a generated nickname, an avatar and numbers. Children can't contact each other.
- **No names in play statistics.** Stats use random ids.
- **No punishment for mistakes.** Wrong answers cost nothing: no lost lives or coins. Mistakes come back later as a "second chance".
- **No timers by default.** A speed bonus exists, but a grown-up has to switch it on, and it only adds points.
- **No streak guilt.** The pet gets sleepy, never sad, and streak freezes cover missed days.
- **Not a replacement for teaching.** Lessons are short refreshers, not a full course.
- **No other curricula yet.** The game follows Cambridge only.
